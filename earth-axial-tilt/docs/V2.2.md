# v2.2 — Uranus / directed-spin illumination

## Delivered scope

`planet-explorer.html` adds side-by-side schematic spheres, scaled Kepler orbit
insets, selected-latitude illumination curves, custom 0–180° tilts, native
Earth/Mars/Uranus selections, English/Japanese Large, explicit playback, and
versioned settings. The original `planet-lab.html` and all Earth v1.x workspaces
retain their accepted semantics. The new page is linked from the old comparison.

The Earth90/Uranus recipe contrasts a hypothetical sideways Earth with the native
97.77° Uranus definition. It changes a copy, never the frozen planet registry.

## Scientific convention

In the explorer's frame +Z is the orbit normal, the Sun direction at Ls is
`[cos(Ls), sin(Ls), 0]`, and the directed spin pole is
`[0, sin(epsilon), cos(epsilon)]`. Body rotation is always right-handed around
that directed pole. Tilt >90° already makes the rotation retrograde relative to
the orbit: do **not** negate the spin a second time.

Positive latitude is toward this spin pole. For Uranus it is opposite the IAU
cartographic north pole. The UI labels the pole with `+`, not geographic N, and
explains the difference. Spring means increasing illumination of the positive
hemisphere, with Ls 0/90/180/270. It is not a real-world dated equinox.

At tilt 97.77°, maximum solar declination is 82.23°, not 97.77°. Frozen rotational
illumination at +65° is 100% at Ls90 and 0% at Ls270. At the exact all-day horizon,
50% is only the historical reporting convention; horizontal energy is zero.

The shaded sphere, surface graticules, selected point, subsolar marker and readouts
use the same vector geometry. Colours/radii and the equatorial ring are schematic;
there are no photo textures, real ring dimensions, cloud dynamics or terrain.

## Constants and sources, checked 2026-10-02

- [JPL approximate elements, Table 1](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html):
  fixed a=19.18916464 au, e=0.04725744; I=.77263783°, node=74.01692503°,
  heliocentric perihelion longitude=170.95427630°. These are fitted reference
  elements, not universal mean elements or a live ephemeris.
- [JPL physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html):
  P=84.016846 Julian years, multiplied by 365.25 Earth days/year in this model.
- [NAIF PCK00011](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/pck00011.tpc):
  BODY799 pole RA=257.311°, DEC=-15.175°, PM rate=-501.1600928°/Earth day.
  We retain a rounded Voyager/IAU-convention sidereal period of 17.24 Earth hours,
  not a claim that this is the unique/latest atmospheric rotation estimate.
- [NASA Uranus facts](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/facts/): obliquity 97.77°.

The perihelion season 3.4124884286485° is **derived**, not copied from a Sun-centred
perihelion longitude. `audits/planet/pole_convention.py` rotates the PCK pole into
the J2000 ecliptic, flips it to the positive spin pole, and projects it on JPL's
perihelion basis. `atan2(-n·p, -n·q)` gives Ls at perihelion. The same calculation
gives tilt 97.7699973°, consistent with the adopted 97.77°.

Earth/Mars constants are byte-preserved inside their definitions. Their legacy
solvers, schemas and panels are not migrated or rewritten in this milestone.

## Clocks and energy

Season mode aligns Ls and holds spin still. Elapsed mode advances equal Earth days
from each world's **own** spring; it does not describe simultaneous ephemerides.
Spin advances by the positive sidereal rate around the directed pole. Playback
is capped per frame, pauses on hidden tabs, and never restarts automatically.

The plotted lit fraction is a geometric fraction of a rotation with the orbit
held fixed. The energy curve is the horizontal TOA flux averaged over that
frozen rotation, not a real integrated sunrise-to-sunset duration. This distinction
is essential for the next Mercury milestone. Equal-Ls samples are not equal-time
samples and must not be averaged to claim an annual mean. Both curves use the
same absolute units and scale for A and B.

## State / acceptance

Explorer format is an independent bounded `explorer=1&state=...` fragment and
`planet-explorer` JSON envelope. Unknown/duplicate/missing fields, nonfinite
numbers, oversized payloads and out-of-range tilts/locations fail atomically.
Valid legacy `planet=1` Earth/Mars links can be read without inventing new science.
No personal display preference or playback state is imported. All old Earth
formats remain on their original pages.

`tests/planet22.test.ts` checks positive-pole geometry, inverse time maps,
frozen-rotation longitude quadrature, mutable-copy isolation and hostile state.
`e2e/v22.spec.ts` exercises actual browser geometry/readouts, clocks, invalid
state, hidden controls, Japanese Large and blocked storage. No physical-iPhone
or public-deployment acceptance is asserted by automated tests.
