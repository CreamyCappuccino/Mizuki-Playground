# v2.4 — Planet Synthesis

## Accepted target for this chapter

The user approved Uranus → Mercury → a four-world synthesis. The new teaching
chapter is `planet-explorer.html` plus `mercury-lab.html`, with an explicit link
from the original Earth controls. This is **not** a replacement of the original
Earth renderer or completion of the original v2.0 whole-Earth migration roadmap.

Earth/Mars/Uranus/Mercury share the same planetary astronomy interface. The
native-value gallery selects either World A or B without changing other fields.
A native world or a hypothetical0–180° tilt can be compared under equal seasonal
angle, or under equal elapsed Earth days since each world's own spring. Five
questions set complete, paused experiments: Earth90 vs Uranus, Earth/Mars similar
tilts, Earth=Earth, equal365days, and zero-tilt Earth/Mercury with different distances.
A sixth route opens Mercury's dedicated coupled-clock experiment.

The optional gallery and settings are in normal document flow and can be closed.
They never overlay the worlds. Native gallery values do not silently change when
A/B hypothetical tilts change. The original Earth side-panel toggles are retained.

## What the common plots mean

Orthographic geometric spheres, fixed-axis graticules and independently scaled
orbit insets use the same vectors and Kepler radius as numerical readouts. They
are scientific diagrams, **not** terrain imagery, a new free-camera WebGL engine,
or a physically shared solar system. Decorative shading is normalized for
visibility; it does not portray Mercury/Uranus radiant intensity on one scale.
The Sun, orbit and world have independent illustrative sizes.

The selected-point instantaneous elevation and horizontal TOA flux use the
currently displayed spin and orbit together. The lit-fraction and rotation-mean
curves, however, deliberately hold each orbital position fixed while integrating
over rotation. They are not real sunrise-to-sunset durations; on slowly spinning
Mercury they are not a solar-day average. The special Mercury workspace resolves
that changing geometry in time instead. No non-Earth temperature is available.

All time values explicitly use Earth hours or days. A sidereal period is one
inertial spin. A mean solar day is a **net cycle**, not a daytime duration. Uranus
uses a retrograde rate sum; Mercury uses exactly two model years per mean solar
day. Modified tilt experiments retain native inertial rotation magnitude, but do
not display a fictional recomputed mean solar-day period. The gallery reports
native values only. Earth retains its accepted historical365-day model year.

The seasonal curves sample181equal-angle locations0..360°, including both endpoints.
They use identical absolute y-scales per metric for A/B; Uranus therefore can be
small beside Mercury without per-world normalization. Numeric readouts remain
available. A chart cursor follows A's Ls in elapsed-time mode; B's angle remains
visible in B's card and inset, and is not silently reset to A's season.

The native table's annual ray-normal mean is analytic:

```
mean S = 1361 / (a^2 sqrt(1-e^2))
```

It is a time-weighted orbital mean, not the simple average of equal-angle samples,
not a planet-area mean (which would divide by4), and not surface/weather forcing.
Its values are independently checked by1024equal-time midpoint samples per planet.

## Persistence and exports

Explorer schema1 remains strict and bounded; it owns A/B world+tilt, clock,
season/day, latitude/longitude, and no camera/playback/display preferences.
The original five-field `planet=1` Earth/Mars comparison imports without changing
scientific meanings. Original main Earth schemas1–4, Feedback and Synthesis stay
with their existing implementations. They are not converted to a lossy format.
Unknown/malformed links and invalid drafts retain accepted results. Restoring
always pauses. A shared link is a snapshot and explicitly becomes stale after
presets, timeline movement or playback. Clipboard denial leaves a selectable URL.

Exports are browser-local; nothing is uploaded:

- Native table CSV: four rows, explicit Earth-time, au, W/m² and capability fields.
- Seasonal comparison CSV:181rows per world; includes world/planet/tilt/latitude,
  Ls, elapsed phase days, radius, ray-normal flux, frozen lit fraction and mean.
- Mercury time-series CSV:721samples over2orbits with coupled spin, solar drift,
  elevation and instantaneous horizontal TOA flux.
- Explorer settings JSON: the exact validated experiment, never cached solutions.

Angular series endpoints0° and360° are the same geometry, with wrapped phase0.
They must not be counted twice when deriving means. Separate native/seasonal/time
exports are intentional: no cross-interpretation of angle as time is implied.

## Science sources and boundaries

All new numerical constants and frame derivations are linked in [V2.2](V2.2.md)
and [V2.3](V2.3.md). Earth/Mars retain [v2.1 provenance](V2.1-HARDENING.md).
`audits/planet/pole_convention.py` reproduces the reference-pole orientations.
No astronomy or temperature constants are fitted to produce a desired picture.

Not implemented: dated ephemerides, mutual planetary perturbations, precession,
libration, terrain/climate of other planets, new atmospheric physics, Venus,
physical-iPhone acceptance, or new public deployment. Those are not implied by
this chapter's four-world completion. Existing original Earth functionality is
kept rather than described as migrated.
