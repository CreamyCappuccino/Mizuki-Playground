# v1.3 - Climate Geography Lab

Feature track: **1.3.0-alpha.1**. This document defines the first verified
v1.3 slice. It does not claim a completed geographic Earth, a 2D GCM, or a
physical iPhone Safari acceptance.

## First slice: idealized land/ocean contrast

The existing Earth Classic model remains a dry, zonally averaged 90-band EBM
with one user-selected global heat capacity. Its equations, coefficients and
Fast/Mixed/Slow results are regression baselines.

The first geography slice adds two explicitly idealized material profiles:

- **Idealized land:** reuses the existing Fast response, 2.5 m equivalent
  water depth.
- **Idealized ocean:** reuses the existing Slow response, 50 m equivalent
  water depth.

They use the same latitude, orbit, tilt, albedo, radiation and meridional
transport. Only effective heat capacity differs. This makes seasonal
temperature amplitude and peak lag inspectable without inventing a longitude
map or silently changing the inherited coefficients.

The global heat-storage selector remains the Earth Classic control. It is
retained but inactive while an idealized material profile is selected. The
selected idealized profile drives the globe, temperature readout, annual
curve, Compare A/B and Atlas. When A/B Compare is closed, the annual dashed
curve uses the other idealized material at the same tilt and orbit. While A/B
Compare is open, that dashed annual curve instead represents world B, and both
worlds share the selected material so tilt/orbit remain the compared
variables. The Atlas difference view always uses the other idealized material,
not B.

Every thermal request, solution, cache key and readiness check carries the
climate profile. A result from Classic, land or ocean must never be shown as a
different profile. Portable state advances to schema 3; schemas 1 and 2 migrate
to Earth Classic, and invalid or unknown payloads remain atomic failures.

## Why the real Earth mask is deferred

The next physics/data milestone is a [separate geography design candidate](DESIGN-v1.3-earth-geography.md).
Its grid replaces the initially proposed Classic-grid reuse after a reported
polar convergence failure. It is not implemented; independent verification and
science review precede any UI connection. Classic remains unchanged.

Natural Earth is a credible future mask source: its vector and raster data are
public domain, its physical vectors include land polygons at 1:110m, 1:50m and
1:10m scales, and its documented coordinate system is geographic WGS84. The
current climate solver, however, has no longitude dimension. Applying a mask
only to the globe texture would make a visual 2D map claim a climate model that
still transports and solves temperature only by latitude.

This alpha therefore records Natural Earth as a candidate rather than bundling
it. A later slice must first define longitude resolution, coastal-cell
classification, area weighting, transport boundaries, data version and a
reproducible vector-to-mask build. Until then, no texture colours or rendered
pixels are used to infer land, ocean or altitude.

Primary source checks:

- Natural Earth terms: <https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/>
- Natural Earth 1:110m physical vectors: <https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/110m-physical-vectors/>
- Natural Earth coordinate-system documentation: <https://www.naturalearthdata.com/features/>

## Verification contract

1. Earth Classic scalar and complete thermal arrays remain unchanged.
2. Land and ocean use identical forcing but distinct effective heat capacity;
   the land annual range is larger and its warm-season peak occurs earlier at
   a tested mid-latitude site.
3. Climate profile participates in worker cache/provenance, stale-reply
   rejection, A/B equality, Atlas readiness and schema round trips.
4. Japanese/English, Large text, mobile layout, Focus, Compare and Orbit remain
   usable. Browser screenshots are inspected rather than blindly accepted.
5. Chromium, macOS Playwright WebKit and same-HEAD CI are separate evidence.
   Playwright WebKit is not a physical iPhone Safari test.
